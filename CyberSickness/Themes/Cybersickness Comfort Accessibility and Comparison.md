# Cybersickness, comfort, accessibility, and XR/non-XR comparison

[[00 CyberSickness Hub|Back to hub]]  /  [[02 Theme Map]]  /  [[Sources/Evidence Register]]

> [!summary] Interpretation
> Cybersickness is not the archive's most frequent subject; it is the safety, accessibility, and measurement layer that decides when headset XR is appropriate, when shared projection is preferable, and how immersive claims should be evaluated.

## What the history says

- The archive progresses from general comfort concern to explicit comparison designs, instrument choices, break/exposure logging, and short optional XR doses.
- Accessibility is wider than vestibular symptoms: seating, mobility, prior familiarity, captions, sensory intensity, learner-controlled breaks, PPE, and a viable fallback modality recur as design requirements.
- Shared-room disorientation can be intentional instructional stress, while headset-induced vestibular symptoms are adverse effects. A useful protocol records both instead of flattening them into a single comfort score.
- The archive contains many proposed measures but little evidence that a validated sickness instrument was actually deployed. That gap makes a small common measurement bundle valuable.

## Current reality check

- [Systematic review of cybersickness measurement instruments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41517296/) -- A 2025 systematic review reports fragmented measurement practice and identifies instrument-quality gaps, especially for clinical populations. Accessed 2026-07-30. (peer-reviewed review indexed by PubMed)
- [Meta-analysis of washout periods in cybersickness research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42308064/) -- A 2026 meta-analysis makes recovery and washout timing a protocol-design concern rather than an informal afterthought. Accessed 2026-07-30. (peer-reviewed meta-analysis indexed by PubMed)
- [HMD content and exposure factors associated with cybersickness](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32300295/) -- Published evidence associates sickness with content, locomotion, and exposure duration, supporting explicit media and exposure logging. Accessed 2026-07-30. (peer-reviewed article indexed by PubMed)
- [SSH Fund 2026 Discovery Award instructions](https://ssih.org/sites/default/files/2025-08/Instructions_DiscoveryAward_FP.pdf) -- The 2026 instructions provide a funding precedent up to $30,000 and explicitly name XR/non-XR outcomes and cybersickness mitigation; this is not evidence that a current round is open. Accessed 2026-07-30. (official funder instructions)

## Evidence gaps and next tests

- No archive-wide record of validated SSQ, FMS, CSQ-VR, or VRNQ data collected across deployments.
- No agreed stop rule, washout interval, adverse-event escalation path, or cross-modality comfort definition.
- Projected-room and headset conditions need separate exposure and sensory descriptions.
