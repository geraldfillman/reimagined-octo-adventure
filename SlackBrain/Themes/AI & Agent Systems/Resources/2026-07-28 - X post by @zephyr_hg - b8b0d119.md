Nearly everyone using Claude is using the chat box and nothing else. A small group built setups that produce work while they are doing something else. There are nine of them, they take a weekend, and the whole build is plain English. Here is the complete list.

Open your Claude history and scroll back a month.

It is almost certainly a list of questions you asked and answers you read. Useful, and gone the moment you closed the tab.

Now picture the same month with nine setups running underneath it. Work arriving in your folder before you sit down, drafts written against your own notes.

That difference comes down to what you built once and left running.

Here are the nine, in the order they should go in.

## The Two That Everything Else Sits On

Skip these and nothing else in the list works properly.

1. **A folder Claude can see.** One folder on your computer holding the files that describe your work. Claude reads all of it, not one pasted excerpt.
2. **A short brief called CLAUDE md.** A page that tells Claude who you are, what you do, and how you want things done.

Once these exist, you stop re-explaining yourself at the start of every session.

That alone changes what the tool is.

## The Three That Hand Claude Your World

These take the setup from knowing about you to reaching your actual work.

1. **Connectors.** Plug in Drive, Gmail, Calendar, Slack, Notion. Claude pulls from where your work already lives instead of asking you to paste it.
2. **A Project with pinned context.** Lock the files and instructions for one recurring job into a workspace you reopen, so it starts warm every time.
3. **A Skill for the thing you repeat.** Write the steps down once and Claude runs the same job the same way, without you supervising the method.

Three moves, and the tool goes from answering to operating inside your real material.

## The Three That Produce Without You Sitting There

This is the part almost nobody has, and it is where the output shows up.

1. **A scheduled research brief.** Set it to run early. You wake up to a written brief on your topic, waiting in the folder.
2. **A content pipeline.** Research, angles, drafts, review, in one chain you point at a topic and check at the end.
3. **An outreach system.** Find the leads, research them, personalize the message, queue the follow-ups. You read every one before it sends.

These run without you at the keyboard. You set the job, then you judge what came back.

## The One That Keeps It Honest

1. **A weekly review routine.** Once a week, Claude reads what happened across your folder and hands you the state of things.

Without it, the other eight drift and you stop trusting them.

With it, you get a standing report on your own work.

## Why Almost Nobody Has Built Any Of These

All of this is unfamiliar, which feels the same as difficult from the outside.

Most people met Claude as a chat box, and nothing since has told them it can be anything else.

So they get better at asking questions, which improves the answer and never changes the output.

The people running the nine setups spent one weekend building instead of asking.

## Where To Start

All nine sit on the same base. A folder Claude can read, a brief it starts from, and jobs that run on a schedule you set.

That build is what [Claude Mastery](https://zephyrhq.gumroad.com/l/ClaudeMastery/Article?utm_source=Twitter&utm_medium=Article&utm_campaign=137) walks through, setup by setup, from an empty folder to all nine running.

Start with the folder and the brief this weekend. The other seven get easier once those two exist.
